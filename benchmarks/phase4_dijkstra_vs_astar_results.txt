Reading (see docs/BENCHMARK_PLAN.md's Phase 4 addendum for the full discussion):

1. A* expands the same or fewer nodes than Dijkstra at every size tested (9<=9, 20<22,
   45<47, 150<=150) -- the heuristic does provide real, if modest, guidance on these
   graphs.

2. A* is slower in wall-clock time than Dijkstra at every size tested. Each heuristic
   evaluation costs two hohmann::compute() calls (trig + pow), and on these scenarios that
   fixed per-evaluation cost is not repaid by the small reduction in nodes/edges touched.

3. The heuristic-quality ratio falls sharply as scenario size grows (0.167 -> 0.003). This
   is a direct, explicable consequence of the heuristic's design (AStarHeuristic.hpp §1):
   h bounds coast time only, deliberately never wait time. As graphs grow, the optimal
   route accumulates more phasing waits (more hops = more chances to need a long wait for
   a rendezvous window), so true cost grows largely from a term the heuristic correctly
   and deliberately ignores. The bound stays valid (never overestimates -- Dijkstra and A*
   agree exactly on arrival time in every row above) but becomes looser, which is the same
   reason A* stops paying for itself as size grows.

4. These two findings are connected, not independent: a heuristic whose quality ratio is
   already below 0.17 at 10 nodes and falling is not expected to prune aggressively enough
   to repay its own evaluation cost, and it does not, on these particular topologies (a
   backbone climb from lowest to highest altitude plus a handful of skip edges).

Scope and honesty notes:
 - This is 4 scenarios x 1 seed each x 10 reps -- a micro-benchmark, not a statistically
   powered study. It shows one real, reproducible characterisation, not a general verdict
   on A* vs Dijkstra for ORCA's graphs.
 - The topology (chain-like, source=lowest altitude, target=highest altitude) was chosen
   for guaranteed connectivity and reproducibility, not because it is the topology where
   A*'s advantage would be largest. A graph with many dead-end branches the heuristic can
   see are hopeless (e.g. a hub-and-spoke shape, or a much larger fan-out around the
   target) would very plausibly show A* pruning far more aggressively. That scenario is
   not built here; it is a reasonable Phase 5+ extension, not a claim made in this report.
 - No number in this file was hand-derived or estimated -- every figure is copy-pasted
   from one real run of the compiled program named above.
